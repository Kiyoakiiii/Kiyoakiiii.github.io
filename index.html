About

I'm an M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at UC Santa Cruz, where I'm a graduate researcher in the Tactile Manipulation Lab. My current work is teaching a Franka Research 3 arm to transplant tomato seedlings: finding a thin, flexible stem in an RGB-D point cloud and grasping it reliably.

Before that, as a systems engineering intern at Zentek, I built a real-time conversational digital human and an IMU-driven digital twin in NVIDIA Omniverse. I care about the full loop from sensing to action, and about pipelines that stay fast and recover gracefully when things go wrong.

I'm based in San Jose, CA and open to relocation. Feel free to reach out.

Autonomous Tomato Seedling Transplantation

Graduate Researcher · Tactile Manipulation Lab, UC Santa Cruz

RGB-D capture→stem segmentation→3D line fit→grasp planning→execution
  • Demonstrated stem-based acquisition, tray extraction, transport, and placement in initial end-to-end trials on a Franka Research 3 with a fixed ZED 2i camera.
  • Annotated 200+ RGB-D images with plant/session-level splits to prevent leakage; fine-tuned a YOLO segmentation model and skeletonized masks to pick long, low-curvature grasp segments.
  • Recovered 3D stem position and tangent with confidence filtering and RANSAC + PCA line fitting; generated grasps filtered by workspace, inverse kinematics, and gripper–tray clearance.
  • Built a fault-tolerant state machine over franky/libfranka with wrench-based contact detection, re-observation, bounded retries, and retreat.
  • Measured gripper capture tolerance under controlled 3-axis offsets to set localization requirements for perception.
